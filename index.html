    From the interface to the transistor-level boundary
    1. Choose a saved case. Press Play, step a clock edge, or step one recorded event. Combinational values can change between clock edges.
    2. Click a region in the chip diagram. Each connection has a recorded current value. Click it to highlight its source and destinations.
    3. Open A to see all 16 BLKs. Choose a BLK and a weight slot to inspect its 64 × 8 DPU positions. Drag the diagram to pan, or zoom with the mouse wheel.
    4. Click a DPU to inspect the recorded device connections inside it. Unrecorded devices and nodes stay explicitly unknown.
    5. Use Before edge → now to compare a selected signal or gate at the latest source, root, or local BLK edge. Now means the current cursor, which can be inside a propagation interval.
    6. The worked example separates reference arithmetic from recorded PSUM, accumulator and result values. Click a captured plane to jump to its trace event.
    7. Use Locate cell to enter BLK, term, bit and slot. Arrow keys on the focused circuit move between cells.
    8. Add a node or a bus to the waveform pane. Drag the timeline cursor in either direction. Time zoom and circuit zoom are independent.
    Read the colors correctly
    Dark hatched BLKs are off. Gray powered cells have no traced WL/RWL activity at this instant. Blue marks active read/evaluation; amber marks a write. A yellow X is an unknown logical value. “Untraced” means no evidence was saved for that point, not zero.
    What this does not prove
    This is a structural digital model. It does not simulate continuous voltages, metastability, leakage, IR drop, switch sizing, or physical timing closure. Motion along a line explains direction only. It is not a measured propagation animation.
    All selected BLKs share one input precision, weight precision and slot per task. Inputs and weights are unsigned. This design has 16 BLKs with 64 terms each; it does not use the earlier Colonnade pipeline.
